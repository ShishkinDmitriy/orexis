# What it is

`market:Good` — what changes hands: what a [lot](/domain/lot.md) is a quantity OF. **Not a property**: a property is what a buyer hopes the
good moves, and one good honestly has several valuations, one per kind of recipient — a
litre raises a pot's `SoilMoisture` *and* a barrel's `StoredLitres`, by different conversion
terms. The domain declares its individuals (`water:Water` is the one shipped); the market
only demands that a source names one.

# The [venue](/domain/venue.md) tie

The good is the join point the cross-join died at (#198). A source states its good ONCE, at
class level — every `WaterSource` vends water, entailed, the datasheet pattern — and each
valuation term states `market:ofGood` beside `market:aboutProperty` and `market:direction`:
one sentence, three parts — a lot of THIS GOOD, priced in THIS PROPERTY, moves it THIS WAY.
Every denomination join closes `venue → marketFor → source → supplies → good ← ofGood ←
valuation`, plus the asker's own stake: the participation rule, the bidder, the reflex, the
menu and the host all walk it, so two opposite levers on one property each carry their own
direction and never each other's. See
[the-lot-states-its-good](/decisions/the-lot-states-its-good.md).

# What it keeps expressible

A market for something no instrument measures — a time slot, a right of way — stays
declarable: the good is not observable, only wanted-about. And a second domain brings its own
good and valuations without touching a line of any join, which is what "the domain is a
plug-in" demands of the market.
