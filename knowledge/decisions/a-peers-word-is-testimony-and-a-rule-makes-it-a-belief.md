# Context

`heard` believes a peer's document as it stands: each graph goes into the belief base as the kind
the document declares, a market call, round, bid, claim or presentation, and is read by every reader
of that kind as it would read the agent's own state
([speech](/domain/speech/speech.md)). What keeps a peer from saying more is one gate at the writer:
every graph must be a kind beneath `orexis:StateGraph`, and one of a name already held must have
arrived `orexis:Received`.

[the-state-is-what-a-scope-can-change-and-everything-known-is-belief](/decisions/the-state-is-what-a-scope-can-change-and-everything-known-is-belief.md)
folds that kind into `orexis:BeliefGraph`, and the gate loses its footing: the ontology, the world,
the society and the self are beliefs too. The record moved the gate to the readers, every reader of
what defines the agent taking only what arrived asserted. Slice 3 of #953 keeps it at the writer for
now, a kind beneath `orexis:BeliefGraph` and not beneath `orexis:PublicGraph`, which is today's safety
with the state kind gone.

Both shapes leave the same thing standing: a peer's document is read as belief by whatever reads its
kind. A peer saying `:bed climate:soil climate:Dry` in a graph it types as one of the market's would be
read as the agent's own state of its bed by every met-test and precondition, though no rule of the
agent's ever judged it. Asked which gate, the sovereign answered neither: what is heard from another
agent should be processed as a sensor's reading is, made either a belief or a prediction.

# The decision

**A peer's document is testimony, in a kind of speech's own.** `heard` puts each graph of the document
into a graph of a kind the speech package declares, beneath `orexis:Graph` alone, as sensing keeps an
observation beneath `orexis:Graph` alone
([an-observation-is-a-percept-and-the-mind-reads-only-beliefs](/decisions/an-observation-is-a-percept-and-the-mind-reads-only-beliefs.md)).
No reader of the mind is handed it. What the peer said its graph is, its kind and its period, is part
of what it said, and stays with it.

**Its arrival is testimony, and takes a reading's path.** It arrives `orexis:Received`, so revision
runs the rules the agent holds over it and every transition is triggered by it once
([transition](/domain/belief/transition.md)). What they conclude is the only thing the mind reads of
it:

- **a belief**, what an inference concludes of the one document, gone when it is said again, as the
  market's rules already conclude a venue open from a round, or what a transition makes of it in the
  agent's own state;
- **a prediction**, what a drift accumulates from what was said, as a drift reads a
  [committed step](/domain/execution/committed-step.md) at an instant: a peer's announced route is the
  peer's word about where it will be, and where the agent then expects it is the drift's.

**The agent's own saying takes the same path.** `said` puts what the agent told a peer into the same
kind, arrived `orexis:Recorded`, and the same rules conclude of it: the round a host opened is open to
the host because its rule says so of the round it said, as it is to its bidders of the round they
heard.

**No gate on kinds remains.** A peer's document may declare any kind; whatever it declares, it is a
graph no reader of the mind is handed. It becomes an action, a rule, a desire or a world only where a
rule the agent holds makes it one, and no shipped rule does. `heard` keeps refusing what it refuses
for other reasons: a document that is none, one saying whose a graph is or who the self is
(`refuse_the_sovereigns`), and a graph of a name that arrived another way.

# Why

**What a peer says is evidence, as a reading is.** A sensor reports a number and the agent's rules
decide what the bed is; a peer reports a round and the agent's rules should decide what the market
is. Believing the document as it stands lets the sender decide what the receiver believes, which is
the one decision this project keeps with the receiver: a transition says what a reading makes of the
state, and nothing else does.

**It is the stack record's rule, mechanised.** A message is a speech act and not an observation, and
the two take different paths through translation
([the-agent-stack-is-a-second-axis](/decisions/the-agent-stack-is-a-second-axis.md)). The paths stay
two, a document and a number; what they share is the seam they cross, revision and the transitions,
which is where the stack record put the BRF row.

**Beliefs about other agents stay first-order.** What is kept is what the peer said, as it said it;
what the agent believes of the world because of it is a conclusion of the agent's rules, kept apart
and gone when the peer says the document again.

**A gate held by structure needs no list.** The writer's gate is a list of kinds a peer may declare,
and the readers' gate a rule some forty readers must each keep; one kind added beneath the wrong
class, or one reader that forgets, is a hole, found by nothing. A kind no reader is handed has no
hole of that shape.

# What was refused

- **The writer's gate on the kind a peer declares**: today's `orexis:StateGraph`, and slice 3's
  interim, a belief and not public. It trusts the sender's typing of its own graph, and what passes
  it is read as the agent's own state, unjudged.
- **The readers' gate on arrival**, #952's plan: every reader of the ontology, actions, drifts,
  rules, shapes, constraints, desires and the self taking only what arrived asserted. It closes
  redefinition and leaves the rest: a peer's word in a kind no such reader names is still read as
  belief, unjudged. It also asks every such reader to keep the rule.
- **The peer's word as an observation of sensing's**: one kind for both, a document heard written as
  a `sosa:Observation`. A speech act has a sender and says what was said, not what an instrument
  measured; the stack record keeps the two paths apart, and here they share the seam and not the kind.

# Seams left open

- **Which peer said it.** The transport does not say who spoke, so a document heard is the word of
  whoever can publish on the agent's topic. A rule that trusts one peer and not another needs the
  sender on the testimony, which is what signing gives; until then a rule trusts what was said on
  the topic. **The trigger: signing.**
- **A rule that concludes a kind of graph.** A peer that should be able to hand the agent an action or
  a desire, an employer giving an employee a task, would need a rule that concludes one; no shipped
  rule does, and [a-role-is-an-instance-an-agent-plays](/decisions/a-role-is-an-instance-an-agent-plays.md)
  leaves the same case open for a role. **The trigger: the first world where one agent's word gives
  another something to do.**

# How it is built

An issue holds the build, after slice 3 of #953. Speech's kind and its page first, with `heard` and
`said` writing it and the interim gate gone; then the market's rules concluding what its actions'
preconditions, effects and implementations read of a call, a round, a bid, a claim and a presentation,
where today they read the documents themselves; then a peer's announced route read by a drift, which
the dispatcher's tests lay by hand today. A world in which a peer's document declares an action graph,
is believed as what was said, and is read by no planner is the test that it holds.

Once built, this supersedes in part the speech page (believed as it stands), the AGENTS line "a peer's
word is a document, believed as it stands where it is state", and the readers' gate in
[the-state-is-what-a-scope-can-change-and-everything-known-is-belief](/decisions/the-state-is-what-a-scope-can-change-and-everything-known-is-belief.md),
whose slice 4 then folds `orexis:PublicGraph` with no gate to move.
