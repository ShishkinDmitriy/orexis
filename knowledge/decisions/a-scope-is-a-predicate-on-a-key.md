# The claim

[scope](/domain/planning/scope.md) said what a bulkhead is for — two wants in different scopes cannot
contradict, so they are planned apart and their plans concatenated — and then named its own limit:
a scope was a set of PREDICATES, and two vans in one courier vocabulary, or a pump and a heater
both writing `sensing:below`, were one scope however little one reached the other. #593 waited on
a scope over variables, and this is what that came to.

An ATOM is a predicate on a key. The key is what the WORLD alone says of the subject a pattern is
about: its own value where the precondition binds the subject from public facts — a disk, a venue,
the agent — and otherwise the values the filling binds alongside it, a reading's feature and its
property, which is exactly how sensing keys an observation. `footprint.atoms_of` reads it by asking
each action's precondition over the public graphs with every pattern OPTIONAL and no filter: what
the world states binds, what the state would have bound stays unbound, and each row is one filling
as far as the world alone decides it. A filling joins the atoms it reads of what some action writes
and the atoms it writes; `scope_actions` partitions the atoms; a subject keyed by nothing keys every
atom of its predicate, which is the predicate partition, the safe side.

What a scope then holds as members: every action a filling of which falls in it, in as many scopes
as its fillings do; and each predicate and each term a key holds, where that is ONE scope and
nowhere where it is two — a reader told nothing joins every group, as it always has. The derivation
clusters a desire's witnesses by the scope of what each is about, a property term; the planner
places a want by the predicates its met-test reads and, where those place it nowhere because two
scopes' actions write them, by the terms the met-test names — and only then.

# What was refused

- **A declaration of what an action moves** — `orexis:moves climate:SoilMoisture` on the dose, as a
  drift says `prediction:moves`. It would have split the greenhouse in an afternoon, and it is the
  thing [footprint](/domain/planning/footprint.md) refuses on its first page: a second statement of
  what the texts already settle, drifting from them with nothing to say so. The precondition says
  the pump's reading is of the property the pump actuates; a declaration would say it again.
- **The partition computed from the candidates a pass admits.** #565 first wrote "computed from the
  graph at pass time", and the candidates the present ground admits carry their fillings with the
  state bound too. But a candidate is admitted only where its precondition holds NOW: a dose is
  admitted when the bed is below, and when it is not there is no candidate to read a key off, so a
  bed that dries later would be clustered with the air until it crossed. The imaginarium outlives
  the pass and is one per scope, so a partition that moved with the state would move the cones
  under the worlds. The public half of a precondition holds whatever the state does.
- **A key named by its variable.** The first cut keyed an atom by `(variable, value)` pairs, and
  hanoi split into a scope per disk: the move of `?disk` reads `?other hanoi:on`, and the other
  disk's atom under another name never met its own. A key is VALUES, and a subject's own value
  where it is bound, so the atom a filling reads of disk two is the atom disk two's own filling
  writes.
- **Terms before predicates.** Placed by every term its met-test names, the puzzle's want — the
  disks on `PegC` — went to the courier's imaginarium, since a peg is a cell the van drives to and
  that term is a key of the courier's atoms alone; the tower hung searching for a disk among drives.
  Predicates place a want where they can; terms break the tie the shared predicates leave.

# Measured

- The greenhouse boots to two scopes, `{SoilMoisture, Dosing}` and `{AirTemperature, Heating}`,
  with the shared `sensing:below` and `sensing:inside` and the bed in neither. A cold dry bed is two
  wants in two imaginaria of one world each where it was one want over four worlds; the pass costs
  107 ms against 58, the whole difference the second imaginarium's filling
  ([measure-the-search](/runbooks/measure-the-search.md)).
- The tower still splits in two, by its words as before, with the pegs now members of the courier's
  scope as terms and `hanoi:on` of the puzzle's.
- Every other shipped world and every stored scope case partitions as it did: their actions bind
  no public subject, so every atom is keyed by nothing, and the predicate partition is what falls
  out.

# Seams left open

- **Two instances of one property.** Two beds each with a pump are two scopes by key, and a desire
  about both beds' soil has witnesses about the one term, `SoilMoisture`, held by both scopes and so
  by neither: the two beds are one want, planned in the first scope. A witness's instance — the bed
  — would place it; the derivation's witnesses carry the focus node, which is the agent, not the bed.
  Two vans wait on the same thing, which is #565's.
- **A derivation's edges are predicates and join every key of theirs.** No derivation rows are
  written in 0.2.0 — `stored_edges` reads an empty graph — so nothing is joined by them today; the
  day one is, a rule reading one subject's reading and writing another's should join across keys
  and a same-subject rule should not, and nothing tells the two apart yet.
- **A key is one hop.** The values keying a subject are those sharing a pattern with it; a reading
  keyed through a sample of the bed (`?reading sosa:hasFeatureOfInterest ?sample . ?sample
  sosa:isSampleOf ?bed`) is keyed by the sample, which is as good a key while samples are not
  shared.
- **The second imaginarium's price.** A scope costs a copy of the beliefs per pass. The world that
  pays more for the copies than it saves in worlds has not been built; the tower and the greenhouse
  both come out ahead or even.
